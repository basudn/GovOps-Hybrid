# GovOps Governance

GovOps operates as a Community Specification Working Group under OWASP. This document describes
the roles, decision-making process, and specification lifecycle that govern the project.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Maintainer** | Organizes activities around developing, maintaining, and updating the specification. Determines consensus and coordinates appeals. Each Working Group designates one or more Maintainers, who may be replaced by Working Group Approval. |
| **Editor** | Ensures the document's contents accurately reflect decisions the group has made, and that the specification follows the formatting and content conventions in `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Each Working Group designates an Editor, replaceable by Working Group Approval. Editors are required to hold an active OWASP membership (per architecture coordination call, Aug 27 2026 — see `docs/07-project-and-roadmap/README.md#release-and-versioning-policy`). |
| **Participant** | Anyone who has made a Contribution to the Working Group subject to its license terms. |

## Decision making

**Consensus-based.** The Working Group makes decisions through an "Approval" (consensus) process.
Agreement from every Participant is preferred but not required — the Maintainer determines
consensus in good faith, considering the dominant view of Participants and the nature of any
support or objections, and documents the evidence for that consensus.

**Appeals.** A decision may be appealed via a pull request or issue. The Maintainer considers the
appeal in good faith and responds in writing within a reasonable time.

## Due-process principles

Adapted from ANSI's Essential Requirements for Due Process, the Working Group adheres to:

- **Openness.** Participation is open to anyone directly and materially affected by the activity in
  question, without undue financial barriers. Voting is not conditioned on membership in any
  organization other than the Working Group's own parent organization, where applicable.
- **Lack of dominance.** No single interest category, individual, or organization may dominate the
  development process to the exclusion of fair consideration of other viewpoints.
- **Balance.** The process seeks a balance of interests, actively recruiting participants from
  diverse interest categories.
- **Coordination and harmonization.** Good-faith efforts are made to resolve conflicts between
  GovOps deliverables and existing industry standards (see `docs/01-explanation/positioning/`
  for how this is applied to Beyond Zero, Muse, AuthZen, and SSF).
- **Consideration of views and objections.** Written views and objections from any Participant
  receive prompt consideration.
- **Written procedures.** This document and related process materials are available to any
  interested person.

## Specification lifecycle

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pre-Draft** | Any Participant may submit a proposed initial draft. The Maintainer designates it Pre-Draft. |
| **Draft Specification** | A Pre-Draft becomes a Draft Specification once Approved; it then becomes the basis for further work. |
| **Approved Specification** | Once the Working Group believes a Draft Specification has met its scope's objectives, it Approves it as an Approved Specification. |

Publication of an Approved Specification includes the terms under which it is made available.
Submission of any Draft or Approved Specification to another standards body requires Working Group
Approval; Participants who developed that material agree to grant the copyright rights necessary
for such submission.

## Where this sits relative to OWASP

GovOps follows OWASP project governance norms in addition to the Community Specification process
above; where the two are not yet reconciled, that is a named gap — see
`docs/07-project-and-roadmap/README.md#open-questions`.
